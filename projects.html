Who benefits from each training approach?

Balanced training gives demographic classes more equal representation. Population-weighted training follows the population distribution. Explore how the tradeoff changes across groups.

Difference in Brier score: population-weighted minus balanced. Lower Brier scores mean better probability predictions. Positive differences favor balanced training; negative differences favor population-weighted training.

Data and interpretation

Values are transcribed from Table 23 (page 39) of our report, rounded to three decimals. These are subgroup differences, not absolute scores or causal effects. No uncertainty intervals are reported in this table. A gain for one group does not establish that a model is unbiased overall.

Data and interpretation

Five-year averages from my growth-accounting notebook using Penn World Table data. Growth rates are annual log differences multiplied by 100, an approximation to percentage growth. Values are rounded to one decimal, so contributions may not add exactly to output growth. Country coverage can differ, and boundary periods may contain fewer than five observations. Residual productivity includes factors the model does not explain; it is not a pure measure of technology. Human capital is outside this decomposition.
